The communication gap

Communication should never become a barrier to being heard.

For millions of people in India, communicating across the gap between Indian Sign Language and spoken language can still mean depending on another person to interpret everyday conversations.

Only an estimated 250–325 certified ISL interpreters serve the country — roughly one interpreter for every 60,000 Deaf people. While authorised institutes offer training in sign-language interpretation, the shortage remains significant.

Why this matters

The problem is not a lack of communication. It is a lack of access.

At a hospital, classroom, workplace, service counter or even a family gathering, a simple conversation can become difficult when the people involved do not share the same language.

Deaf people should not have to wait for an interpreter to ask a question, explain a need or participate in an everyday moment. Communication should be available when the moment happens.

Why we built SignSpeak

We wanted technology to make the conversation easier — in both directions.

SignSpeak started with a simple idea: if technology can understand spoken language, recognise gestures and generate natural language, it should also be able to help people communicate through sign.

Instead of building only a sign-to-speech translator, we wanted to create a two-way bridge — one that can recognise ISL and speak the response, while also helping a hearing person learn and communicate back through sign.

Independence Communicate without always relying on an interpreter. Inclusion Keep both sides of the conversation involved. Learning Make it easier for anyone to practise signing.

About SignSpeak

A two-way bridge between Indian Sign Language and spoken conversation.

SignSpeak is a browser-based Indian Sign Language translator designed to support communication in both directions. A webcam can capture signs and convert recognised gestures into spoken language, while spoken input can be converted into corresponding sign animations through a 3D avatar.

ISL → Speech

The system captures hand, face and body movements through the webcam, recognises trained ISL gestures and converts the recognised words into a natural spoken sentence.

Speech → ISL

Spoken language is transcribed in the browser and matched with supported signed phrases, which are then performed by a rigged 3D avatar.

Behind the technology

What happens behind the camera

Every frame from the webcam is processed using MediaPipe Holistic to extract hand, face and pose keypoints. These keypoints are passed to an LSTM model trained to recognise ISL gestures.

Once the signs are recognised, the resulting keywords are combined into a more natural sentence using Gemini before the sentence is spoken aloud through the browser.

In the reverse direction, speech is transcribed using the Web Speech API and matched against a library of supported signed phrases. A Three.js-based 3D avatar then performs the corresponding sign.

What we believe

Understanding should not depend on which side of the conversation you are on.

Sign language is often treated as something only Deaf people need to learn. We believe communication works better when both sides make an effort to understand each other.

Every person deserves the opportunity to express themselves, participate and be understood. That is why SignSpeak is not only about translation. Practice Mode is designed to give hearing users a way to learn supported signs and communicate back.
